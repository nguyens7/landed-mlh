# Landed speech: transcription and parsing results

Generated by `uv run python eval.py --asr gemini`. ASR: Gemini `gemini-3.8-flash` (primary) and OpenAI `gpt-4o-transcribe` (cache only). Parser fallback LLM: Gemini `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` with a response schema. TTS: Gemini `gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts` (Charon, Kore, Fenrir) plus an extra OpenAI voice set `gpt-4o-mini-tts` (onyx, nova, ash).

Synthetic sets: Gemini-TTS 400 clips, OpenAI-TTS 384 clips (Gemini ASR) / 384 clips (OpenAI ASR), from 50 scripted utterances. ATCO2: 100 real clips.

**Read these numbers with care.** The grammar and the NYC vocabulary prompt were written against the same script bank the synthetic clips come from, so parsing scores on synthetic clips are an optimistic, in-distribution bound. The channel is simulated (band-pass, clipping, hiss, rotor bed), not real VHF. Only the ATCO2 section uses real recordings, and those are European ATC, not NYC helicopter traffic. Neither TTS provider is guaranteed to say exactly the script (WER includes TTS slips).

## 3. Does `asr_confidence` predict errors?

Gemini 3.x exposes no token logprobs, so `asr_confidence` is built from `audio_quality x (1 - uncertain word share)` as self-reported by the model (`conf_self`) times agreement between the transcript and two temperature-1 resamples (`conf_consistency`). Signal comparison:

| signal | AUROC (end-to-end correct) | AUROC (WER<=10%) | Spearman vs WER |
|---|---|---|---|
| conf_self | 0.908 | 0.874 | -0.704 |
| conf_consistency | 0.937 | 0.914 | -0.804 |
| product (asr_confidence) | 0.939 | 0.922 | -0.763 |

For reference, OpenAI's logprob confidence on its clips: AUROC 0.933 (end-to-end), Spearman vs WER -0.560.

## 5. Suggested ASR-confidence threshold

* Headline synthetic set, best Youden-J threshold **0.80** (AUROC 0.939); WER>10% target: **0.80**.
* ATCO2 (real audio, WER>25% target): best Youden-J threshold **0.60** (AUROC 0.882).
* Rule used: the lowest t that catches at least 65% of errors on both sets. **Suggested threshold: 0.60**. It hands off 16.5% of synthetic clips (catching 65.9% of critical errors) and 48.0% of ATCO2 clips (catching 71.7% of bad transcripts).
