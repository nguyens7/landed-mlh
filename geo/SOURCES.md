## OSM water relations (ODbL 1.0, (c) OpenStreetMap contributors)

`geo/fetch_water.py` assembles these into `geo/water_hudson.geojson` / `geo/water_eastriver.geojson`, which `build.py` reads (no network calls at build time). Fetched via the public Overpass API (`overpass-api.de`), which required a `Referer: https://overpass-turbo.eu/` header to avoid an HTTP 406, and was frequently saturated (many HTTP 504s, several retries per relation):

| Relation ID | Tags | Covers (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 2389611 | natural=water, water=river, tidal=yes | Hudson River, ~40.707N-40.795N |
| 2389521 | natural=water, water=river | Hudson River, ~40.791N-40.884N |
| 2389633 | natural=water, water=river, waterway:name=East River | East River, ~40.688N-40.748N |
| 2389563 | natural=water, water=river, name="West Channel" | West side of Roosevelt Island |
| 2389581 | natural=water, water=river, name="East Channel" | East side of Roosevelt Island |
| 20805948 | natural=water, water=river, waterway:name=East River | East River, ~40.773N-40.798N |

Two other approaches were tried and abandoned (documented for anyone picking this up later): (1) OSM administrative-boundary polygons for Manhattan/Queens/Brooklyn/NJ towns (via Nominatim, cached in `geo/land_union.geojson` by `geo/fetch_land.py`) -- these looked shoreline-accurate in isolated spot checks but turned out to extend partway across the river toward the NY/NJ state line (confirmed: the Manhattan polygon "contains" a point in the middle of the Hudson at 40.75N), so subtracting them produced a corridor that still spilled onto what should have been open water. (2) USGS National Map NHD "Area" hydrography layer -- real federal data, but it does not map the tidal Hudson/East River reaches adjacent to Manhattan as polygon features at all (the unnamed polygons it does return nearby are Hackensack River/Newark Bay/Long Island Sound features, not the Hudson or East River mainstems). Neither is used in the final geometry; `land_union.geojson` is kept only as a (very rough) land reference in `geo/check.py`'s discussion of the land-overlap metric, where its unreliability for that purpose is called out explicitly.

## Why the corridor polygons also carry a `dilate_nm()` tolerance buffer

Even the real OSM water polygons under-covered real 2026-10-10 helicopter traffic (85.1% Hudson / 74.3% East River before dilation) by small margins -- median ~0.2-0.25 nm, driven by ADS-B position noise and by helicopters legitimately operating over piers/structures right at the mapped shoreline that are not themselves mapped as water. `build.py` applies a uniform +0.3 nm (Hudson) / +0.5 nm (East River) buffer to the final corridor polygon to reach the >=90% coverage target; see `geo/check.py`'s acceptance numbers for the measured before/after effect.

## Not included / explicitly skipped

- **JFK and EWR protected volumes**: not modeled. SPEC.md marks their runway layouts **[UNVERIFIED]** and the FAA airport diagrams were not pulled in the time available; including placeholder geometry without a source would violate the "never invent coordinates" rule.
- **West 30th St Heliport (JRA)**: skipped per SPEC.md §1.1 ("don't put it on the map until checked on AirNav" -- not checked here), even though it turned up incidentally in an Overpass query (OSM way 160756588, icao=JRA) while fetching water data; not added since it wasn't independently verified against AirNav as the spec requires.
- **Other Manhattan hospital rooftop pads** (Columbia/NYP, Mount Sinai, etc.): skipped, no published FAA record found (per SPEC.md).

## Frequencies

- Hudson CTAF 123.05 and East River CTAF 123.075 come from the FAA SFRA kneeboard (https://www.faasafety.gov/files/gslac/courses/content/79/776/kneeboard.pdf) and the 2010 FAA course. 14 CFR 93.351(c) defers to the frequency "as depicted on the New York VFR Terminal Area Chart (TAC) and/or New York Helicopter Route Chart". **Verify on the current chart** before using these numbers publicly (see #29 item 2). No geo file encodes them.
